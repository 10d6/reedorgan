±2 semitones

Playing gestures from singing, horns and blues harmonica rather than from the reed organ itself. Scoop starts a note below its pitch and slides up into it, landing exactly in tune at the end of Scoop time (or when the reed reaches full voice, if that's later). Every reed on the key, and its coupled octaves, bends together. Top voice scoops only the highest note you're holding, so a melody bends over a steady chord; leaps up scoops it only when it jumps up a minor third or more; by velocity scoops harder-struck notes deeper (mouse and computer keys count as fairly hard). Accents scoops only notes struck at or past the Crossover velocity, the full amount or not at all; mouse and computer keys count as 100. Ease in moves fastest at first and settles; S-curve holds low a moment, then rises, for a more obvious bend; Late snap holds below longest and speeds up all the way into the note.

Vibrato waits for the note to settle, then grows in, as a singer's does: short notes never get it, long ones bloom. Delay counts from when the scoop lands; Never leaves it to a controller. Top voice gives the vibrato to the highest note you're holding and moves it to each new top note. Natural variation makes it start a little slow and speed up as it grows, and lets its rate and width wander slightly, as a voice's do. Voice colour brightens the tone as the pitch rises and darkens it as it falls, like a singer's harmonics passing through the resonances of the mouth.

Delay control: press Learn and move any knob, slider or pedal on a MIDI controller. All the way down is Never, all the way up is no delay, so pushing it brings the vibrato in sooner, even on a note you're already holding. A MIDI keyboard's mod wheel sets Scoop (unless you learn it for the delay), and its pitch wheel bends every sounding note by up to Bend range.

Modulation sequencer
25% · 0.9″

A piano roll of sweeps. Each row is a shape, rising or falling; tap a square to put that shape there, and drag either end of a note to stretch it backwards or forwards. Tap a note to select it: Backspace removes it, Shift with the arrow keys stretches it, Alt with the arrow keys shortens it. One shape plays at a time, so a new note replaces any note it lands on.

Each note moves the wind by Amount, starting from wherever the last note left it, and the wind holds still between notes. A sweep that reaches 0″ or 3.6″ stops there. The pattern keeps time in 16th notes with Pedal autoplay's tempo. It starts on the first note of a phrase (or on each note), loops while you hold keys, starts each pass from the Direct wind slider's setting, and lets the wind ease back to that setting when you let go. Turning it on turns Direct wind on.

Hold ← → (or Space) to work the treadles. Play with Z–M for the low octave and Q–U and up for the middle. Esc silences everything.

Treadles

Easy wind charges the bellows for no more than three notes on a single stop, however much you play, so full chords and extra stops don't need harder pumping. The sound doesn't change. Each treadle feeds air through its own envelope. Pressing starts the attack; holding keeps the sustain flowing; letting go starts the release. Times run up to 30 seconds.

Pedal autoplay

100 bpm

Tap a beat to change it: Rest, Left, Right, or Tie, which holds the stroke before it longer. The pattern loops, starting when you first play. Tempo counts quarter notes, or eighth notes in 6/8. Your own feet still work, and the helper pump adds a trickle on top.

Things to listen for

Let the organ pump itself

Pedal autoplay works the treadles while you play. Hold a big chord and watch the gauge sink between strokes; set the pattern to Right, Left, Right, Left or raise the tempo and the wind holds. Ties make longer, slower strokes.

Easy wind

Draw every stop and hold a big chord: the gauge sinks. Tick Easy wind and it climbs back and stays put. The reeds sound the same; the Use readout shows the air of only three of them.

Let the wind run out

Turn off Pedal autoplay, set the helper low, hold a chord and stop pumping. The sound gets quieter, then darker, and creeps a few cents sharp before the reeds stop speaking.

Pump harder

Strong, alternating strokes push the reeds harder. They get brighter and buzzier, and the pitch sinks a few cents flat. Push too hard and the relief valve vents.

Slow-motion feet

Give a treadle a long attack and release. The organ swells in and fades away on its own, like a very patient player.

The shimmer

Draw Celeste with Diapason. The Celeste reeds are tuned a little sharp so the two ranks beat against each other in a slow wave. Celeste beat sets how fast, the same on every note.

Melody and accompaniment

Divide the stops, then draw soft Melodia in the bass and Diapason with Celeste in the treble. The tune sings out over a quiet accompaniment.

Swell and fan

Vox Humana spins a little fan in front of the reeds. The fundamentals hold steady while the upper harmonics flicker and swirl. Try the Spinning fan shape with some Stereo sweep, and listen to it coast down when you push the stop in. The knee swell opens the lid gradually: about 6 dB louder and brighter, but it drinks wind faster.

Living tuning

Hold a chord with Diapason and Melodia and pump unevenly. Each key drifts at its own pace, so the intervals between keys never settle into one pattern. Fit a new set of reeds to hear a different instrument.

How a reed speaks

Play a low C, then a high one, slowly. The low reed takes a moment to get going and starts almost as a pure hum before its buzz fills in; the high one speaks at once. Low wind makes every reed slower. Tone bloom sets how dark notes start.

Reeds in step

Hold one note with Diapason and Melodia. The two ranks pull together and sound as one steady tone. Set Unison pull to Off and the same note swells and fades slowly as the two reeds drift past each other.

Deep notes

Draw the Bourdon alone and play the bottom of the keyboard. The case rolls off the deepest fundamentals, so the bass sounds reedy, as on a real organ. Set Cabinet bass to Off for a fuller, deeper bass, and choose Full range to play notes below any reed organ from a MIDI keyboard.

Couplers

Draw Sub Octave and hold a chord: the keys an octave down sink with yours, and the bass grows heavy. Every coupled note is another set of reeds drinking wind.

Scoop into the tune

Open Expression and set Scoop to a semitone. Hold a chord in the left hand and play a melody above it: each melody note slides up into pitch and lands dead in tune, while the chord stays put. Try S-curve for a more obvious bend, Top voice, leaps up so only the jumps scoop, or the mod wheel to ride the depth as you play.

Let long notes sing

Open Expression and set Vibrato to about ±30 ¢. Play a quick run, then hold the last note: the run stays plain and the held note settles, then blooms into vibrato. Add a semitone of Scoop and the vibrato waits for each note to land first. Natural variation keeps it from sounding like a machine; Voice colour makes the tone shimmer with it. Set Delay to Never and Learn a knob or pedal to bring the vibrato in yourself.

Let the pattern breathe

Open Modulation sequencer and tick Play the pattern. Hold a chord: the wind swells up and back every bar, in time with Pedal autoplay's tempo, and the gauge follows it. Tap squares to add sweeps of your own, drag their ends to stretch them, and raise Amount for deeper swells. Set Starts on to Each note for a swell that restarts with every chord.

Voice your own organ

Open Rank voicing under Adjustments. Darken the Diapason, brighten the Principal, or detune a rank a few cents to hear how much a regulator's touch changes the instrument.
